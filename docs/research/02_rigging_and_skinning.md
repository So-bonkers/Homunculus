# Research 02: Automatic rigging, skinning and character animation representation

*Written 2026-10-13 for Homunculus. Slice: how a static 3D mesh becomes something that can be animated, and how the field got from hand-tuned geometry code (2007) to transformer models that emit a skeleton and skin weights in seconds (2026).*

*Provenance note: every paper, date, license and hardware figure below was checked against an arXiv abstract, a GitHub README or a Hugging Face model card during this session, unless marked **(unverified)** or **(background knowledge)**. Performance numbers quoted from papers are the authors' own claims. Nothing here was run on the project's AMD ROCm GPU; every learned rigger below documents CUDA, so ROCm compatibility is **unverified** for all of them.*

---

## 0. Vocabulary first (for a newcomer coming from audio)

- **Mesh**: the character's surface as a bag of points (**vertices**) joined into triangles. Think of it as the "waveform"; everything else is metadata about how it may move.
- **Skeleton / armature / rig**: a tree of **joints** (bones). Each joint has a position and a rotation relative to its parent, so rotating the shoulder carries the elbow, wrist and fingers with it. Setting all joint rotations at once is a **pose**; a list of poses over time is an **animation**. A skeleton with its controls is called a **rig**; making one is **rigging**.
- **Skinning**: gluing the mesh to the skeleton so that when bones move, the surface follows. The glue is a table of **skin weights** (also called **blend weights**): for every vertex, a few numbers saying "this vertex follows the upper arm 70%, the forearm 30%". Each vertex's weights sum to 1.
- **LBS (Linear Blend Skinning)**: the standard algorithm. Move each vertex as if it were rigidly attached to each of its bones in turn, then average the results using the weights. It is fast and universal (every game engine does it) but produces the "candy-wrapper" collapse when a joint twists and volume loss at bent elbows. **DQS (dual quaternion skinning)** is a variant that blends rotations better and avoids the collapse, at the cost of a different bulge artefact. **Corrective blend shapes** are small extra sculpts added on top to patch bad joints.
- **Blend shapes / morph targets**: stored alternative versions of the mesh (for example "smile"), mixed with sliders. Mostly used for faces.
- **Weight painting**: the manual way to author skin weights, brushing influence onto the mesh. Slow, skilled, and what the learned methods replace.
- **Retargeting**: taking an animation made for one skeleton and applying it to a different skeleton (different proportions, different bone count, or a totally different creature).
- **IK (inverse kinematics)**: you say "put the hand here" and an algorithm solves the joint angles; the opposite of **FK** (forward kinematics), where you set the angles. IK is how feet get pinned to the ground.
- **Foot skate**: when a character's planted foot slides across the floor instead of staying put. A common artefact of motion captured from video or generated by a model.
- **Rig-free / neural deformation**: moving the mesh (or a 3D Gaussian cloud) directly with a learned function, with no skeleton at all.
- **Formats**: **glTF/GLB** (open, web/game friendly, skinning stored as up to 4 joints per vertex by default), **FBX** (Autodesk's proprietary but ubiquitous exchange format; what Mixamo uses), **USD** (Pixar's scene format, with a skeleton schema called UsdSkel), **VRM** (a glTF-based avatar format with a standard humanoid bone list and spring-bone physics), **BVH** (plain text motion capture: a skeleton plus per-frame joint angles).

## 1. Overview in ten lines

1. For about 15 years (2007 onward) rigging an arbitrary mesh meant fitting a template skeleton by geometry (Pinocchio) and computing skin weights by a physical analogy (heat diffusion), still what Blender's "Automatic Weights" does.
2. In 2020-2021 the first neural riggers (RigNet, Neural Blend Shapes) learned from artist-made rigs, but on small datasets of a few thousand characters and with fragile skeleton topology.
3. In 2024-2025 three things changed at once: much larger rigged datasets (Articulation-XL, Rig-XL, Anymate at 230K), transformers that predict skeletons as token sequences like a language model, and diffusion/attention models that predict skin weights.
4. Make-It-Animatable (CVPR 2025) showed a different trade: fix the skeleton to the Mixamo humanoid and get a sub-second, game-ready result. This is what Homunculus uses.
5. UniRig, MagicArticulate, RigAnything and Puppeteer (all 2025) went template-free: any category (humans, animals, insects), different bone counts, output as a rig file.
6. In 2026 the frontier moved to unifying skeleton and skin into one autoregressive model with reinforcement-learning fine-tuning (SkinTokens/TokenRig, Feb 2026), and to generating geometry and rig together (Rigel3D, Stroke3D).
7. In parallel, rig-free methods (Animate3D, AnimateAnyMesh) animate a mesh straight from text or video, trading editability and engine compatibility for flexibility.
8. Retargeting matured from "same topology only" to learned cross-topology methods (SAN, R2ET, then 2025-26 MoReFlow, Motion2Motion, PALUM), though quadruped-to-humanoid remains research-grade.
9. Hands, faces, cloth and hair are still handled with specialised models (MANO/SMPL-X, FLAME, ARKit blendshapes, spring bones, neural simulators), mostly outside the generic auto-riggers.
10. Practical state in Oct 2026: for humanoids a Mixamo-style rig is solved enough; for everything else (creatures, props, accessories, clothing layers) an open pipeline exists but needs a human check, and licences/datasets are still partly "coming soon".

## 3. Significant developments

### 3.1 Classic: Pinocchio (2007)

**What.** Ilya Baran and Jovan Popovic, "Automatic Rigging and Animation of 3D Characters", SIGGRAPH 2007 (ACM TOG).
**How it works.** Step one is *skeleton embedding*: a generic humanoid skeleton template is fitted inside the mesh by an optimisation that scores candidate joint placements against the shape. Step two is *skin attachment*: each vertex first gets weights based on its proximity to nearby bones, then the weights are smoothed by solving a **diffusion equation** over the surface, the "heat" picture: imagine each bone as a heater and let the warmth spread across the skin; a vertex's weights are the temperature shares from each bone. The paper says the prototype takes under a minute per character.
**Why it mattered.** First complete "mesh in, animatable character out" system. Its heat-style weighting is the ancestor of what Blender does when you parent a mesh to an armature "With Automatic Weights" (one source says Blender implements heat weighting; I could not retrieve Blender's own documentation text for it, so treat the exact algorithm as **partly unverified**). Typical failure: Blender's well-known "bone heat weighting failed" error, from bones with no nearby vertices or a non-uniformly scaled mesh.
**License/hardware.** CPU only. **Open-source:** paper yes; original code was released but I did not verify its licence.
**Link.** https://people.csail.mit.edu/ibaran/papers/2007-SIGGRAPH-Pinocchio.pdf

### 3.2 Classic: Blender Automatic Weights and Rigify, Mixamo

**Blender.** You bind a mesh to an armature (Ctrl-P, "With Automatic Weights"), then fix weights by hand in Weight Paint mode. **Rigify**, bundled with Blender, gives a "human metarig" whose bones you align to your mesh and then "Generate Rig" to get an animator-friendly control rig. Both are free (Blender is GPL) and reliable if the mesh is clean, but need manual alignment per character. Homunculus already uses Blender for baking and texturing, so this is the natural **fallback and repair tool**.
**Mixamo (Adobe).** A free web auto-rigger: upload a humanoid, click on chin, wrists, elbows, knees and groin, get a ~65-bone FBX rig (the Hugging Face Mixamo dataset card for Make-It-Animatable says "mostly standard 65 mixamorig bones" with a range of 42-112). Forum reports show it is fragile on hands: users report hands rigged with only two or three finger chains, and a 2025 thread reports poor results for non-human proportions. Nothing official on its roadmap was found. Its real value is the **huge free clip library**, which is why every Mixamo-compatible rig (including MIA's) is attractive.
**Links.** https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/2.82/animation/armatures/skinning/index.html ; https://community.adobe.com/questions-696/mixamo-auto-rig-messing-up-hands-not-including-all-fingers-589141

### 3.3 RigNet (2020)

**What.** Zhan Xu, Yang Zhou, Evangelos Kalogerakis, Chris Landreth, Karan Singh, "RigNet: Neural Rigging for Articulated Characters", SIGGRAPH 2020, arXiv 2005.00559.
**How.** A graph neural network on the mesh predicts where joints should go (a clustering-style prediction), a second network predicts which joints connect to form the tree, and a third predicts skin weights. It was trained on the ModelsResource-RigNetv1 dataset of about 2.7K rigged characters.
**Why it mattered.** First learned system that produced skeletons with animator-like topology rather than fitting a fixed template; the baseline every later paper compares against.
**License/hardware.** Original repo is zhan-xu/RigNet. Licence reports conflict (one mirror says GPL-3.0, a fork says dual licence): **unverified**; check the original LICENSE before use. Small GPU.
**Link.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00559

### 3.4 Neural Blend Shapes (2021)

**What.** Peizhuo Li et al., "Learning Skeletal Articulations with Neural Blend Shapes", SIGGRAPH 2021, arXiv 2105.02451.
**How.** Two branches: an *envelope* branch predicts skeleton-based skinning for a fixed articulation structure (bipeds, quadrupeds) and deforms the T-posed character; a *residual* branch predicts corrective blend shapes mixed by coefficients that depend on joint rotations, fixing elbow and shoulder artefacts. Trained with indirect supervision (it sees deformed shapes, not ground-truth weights).
**Why.** It treats LBS error as something to learn a correction for, and its output is standard skeleton plus blend shapes, so it works in engines. The idea (pose-dependent correctives) is exactly what is missing in raw LBS.
**License/hardware.** Public PyTorch code (PeizhuoLi/neural-blend-shapes); licence not checked. Small GPU.
**Link.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.02451

### 3.5 Make-It-Animatable (MIA, CVPR 2025) — the current Homunculus rigger

**What.** Zhiyang Guo et al., "Make-It-Animatable: An Efficient Framework for Authoring Animation-Ready 3D Characters", arXiv 2411.18197 (v1 Nov 2024, v3 Mar 2025), CVPR 2025 Highlight.
**How.** One network predicts three things together for a humanoid: the **blend weights**, the **bone positions** and a **pose transform** that brings the character into a canonical T-pose. A particle-based shape autoencoder lets it accept meshes or 3D Gaussian splats; a coarse-to-fine representation and "structure-aware" modelling keep it robust for non-standard proportions. It is trained on Mixamo characters (plus some 3DBiCar samples for odd head-body ratios) and outputs the Mixamo-style skeleton, so Mixamo clips apply directly. The authors report about 0.5 s per ~8k-vertex mesh.
**Why mattered.** Speed and compatibility: a game-ready, Mixamo-compatible rig in under a second, from any pose.
**Notes.** Repo `jasongzy/Make-It-Animatable` is MIT per its sidebar (the HF Space README says Apache-2.0; the two disagree, **verify**). The README says the training default is for an A100 80 GB; no inference VRAM stated; "only mesh inputs are supported" in v2. A **v2** branch exists using the Hunyuan3D 2.1 ShapeVAE backbone with retrained models and a different pose representation (ortho6d plus joint positions), run through `app_v2.py`. The Mixamo training dataset on HF is gated. The README does not mention a "52-bone" count; the Homunculus README says 52 so that is its own configuration (the paper page I read states no bone count). Limitations that matter: humanoid only; nothing documented about finger quality.
**Link.** https://github.com/jasongzy/Make-It-Animatable ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18197

### 3.6 MagicArticulate (CVPR 2025)

**What.** Chaoyue Song et al. (NTU, ByteDance Seed, A*STAR), arXiv 2502.12135.
**How.** Two stages. First an **autoregressive transformer** writes the skeleton as a sequence of tokens (a joint, its parent, the next joint...), handling any number of bones, like an LLM writing a sentence. Second, a **diffusion** model predicts skin weights, guided by geodesic (along-the-surface) distance priors so weights follow body topology rather than straight-line proximity. It introduces **Articulation-XL** (33K models at first; Articulation-XL2.0 now 48K+, filtered from Objaverse-XL).
**Why.** Showed that scale plus sequence modelling beats template fitting for arbitrary categories; its dataset became the training ground for UniRig and Puppeteer.
**License/hardware.** Code Apache-2.0; README says inference needs about 4.6 GB VRAM and 1-2 s per inference; CUDA 11.8 and flash-attn wheels (NVIDIA-oriented). Dataset licence not found: **unverified**.
**Link.** https://github.com/Seed3D/MagicArticulate ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12135

### 3.7 RigAnything (SIGGRAPH 2025 / ACM TOG 44(4))

**What.** Isabella Liu et al. (UCSD, Adobe Research, Hillbot), arXiv 2502.09615.
**How.** A transformer generates joints one at a time starting from the root, in breadth-first order (positions plus parent index), conditioned on the shape; **diffusion modelling** refines joint placement; skin weights are predicted by the same model. Trained on RigNet and Objaverse data; claims humanoids, quadrupeds, insects and marine creatures in seconds.
**Why.** Template-free in the strongest sense: no category assumption at all.
**License/hardware.** I found no confirmed code release: **unverified**. Treat as a paper, not a tool.
**Link.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09615

### 3.8 UniRig (SIGGRAPH 2025)

**What.** Jia-Peng Zhang et al. (Tsinghua, VAST), "One Model to Rig Them All", arXiv 2504.12451.
**How.** An autoregressive model with **Skeleton Tree Tokenization** (a compact way to write a skeleton hierarchy as tokens, so tokens can be shared between branches such as a hand's five fingers) predicts the skeleton, then a **bone-point cross-attention** network predicts skin weights per vertex against the predicted bones. Introduces **Rig-XL**, over 14,000 rigged models, plus VRoid anime characters. Authors claim large gains (215% rigging accuracy, 194% motion accuracy over baselines; unverified by third parties).
**Why.** The first public one-model skeleton plus skinning system covering humans, animals and anime characters; supports VRM input.
**License/hardware.** MIT code. README: at least 8 GB VRAM for generation; skin training needs 60 GB+. Released checkpoints are trained on Articulation-XL2.0; the full Rig-XL/VRoid checkpoints and **bone attribute prediction for physics-style secondary motion** are marked "coming soon". Accepts .obj/.fbx/.glb/.vrm; outputs .fbx. Optional Blender VRM add-on. The README now names SkinTokens as the successor.
**Link.** https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/UniRig ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12451

### 3.9 Puppeteer (NeurIPS 2025 spotlight)

**What.** Chaoyue Song et al., arXiv 2508.10898 (NTU, ByteDance Seed, A*STAR).
**How.** An autoregressive skeleton model (with a joint-based tokenisation and a hierarchical ordering) plus an **attention-based skinning model** with topology-aware joint attention; then **animation by differentiable optimisation**: render the rigged model, compare to a driving video, adjust joint rotations by gradient descent until it matches.
**Why.** Closes the loop from "mesh" to "mesh dancing like this video" for arbitrary categories, which is exactly the Homunculus aim for non-human characters.
**License/hardware.** Apache-2.0; Python 3.10, PyTorch 2.1.1, CUDA 11.8, flash-attn and pytorch3d; no VRAM figure stated. FBX export needs `bpy==4.2.0`. Checkpoints on HF (Seed3D/Puppeteer). The repo carries a 59.4K-sample Articulation-XL2.0 description.
**Link.** https://github.com/Seed3D/Puppeteer ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10898

### 3.10 Anymate (SIGGRAPH 2025 listing)

**What.** Yufan Deng et al., arXiv 2505.06227. A dataset of **230K 3D assets with expert rigging and skinning**, claimed 70x larger than previous ones, and baselines in three modules: joint prediction, connectivity, skin weights.
**Why.** The largest labelled rigging corpus. A 2026 paper (Rigel3D) trains on its ~225K training split.
**License/hardware.** Code and data on GitHub `yfde/Anymate` and a HF demo; **no licence stated** on the repo page; training set up for 8 GPUs. Check data licences (the assets originate from public 3D collections) before commercial use.
**Link.** https://anymate3d.github.io ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06227

### 3.11 Auto-Connect (June 2025)

arXiv 2506.11430. Puts connectivity into the token stream itself (special tokens mark the end of each joint's children and each hierarchy layer) and post-trains with **Direct Preference Optimization** using a topology reward; picks the most influential bones per vertex using geodesic features to cut skinning artefacts. Paper only found; code status unverified. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11430

### 3.12 SkinTokens / TokenRig (Feb 2026) — the newest verified general rigger

**What.** Jia-peng Zhang et al. (Tsinghua, VAST), arXiv 2602.04805, "SkinTokens: A Learned Compact Representation for Unified Autoregressive Rigging".
**How.** Skin weights are very sparse (each vertex follows only a couple of bones), so a **FSQ-CVAE** (a variational autoencoder with finite scalar quantisation) learns a small vocabulary of "skin tokens" that decode into dense per-vertex weights. A single autoregressive transformer (TokenRig, built on the Qwen3-0.6B architecture per the model card) then emits skeleton tokens and skin tokens in one sequence. A **reinforcement-learning stage (GRPO)** with geometric and semantic rewards refines it for unusual assets. Authors claim 98-133% better skinning accuracy and 17-22% better bone prediction than prior methods (their numbers, not independently verified).
**Why.** Skeleton and skin are predicted jointly rather than in two disconnected steps, and RL is used to fix out-of-distribution shapes (creatures, props), which was the weakness of supervised-only riggers. This is the natural next step beyond MIA for non-humanoids. Homunculus already uses SkinTokens to put a non-T-posed input model into a T-pose, so the dependency exists.
**License/hardware.** MIT. Model card: NVIDIA GPU with at least 14 GB for inference; checkpoints total about 1.6 GB; input mesh (example .glb), output .glb. Training data "will be released separately" (not yet). ROCm: unverified.
**Link.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04805 ; https://huggingface.co/vadokxxx/SkinTokens (a mirror; the official listing is VAST-AI/SkinTokens per search results) ; project page https://zjp-shadow.github.io/works/SkinTokens/

### 3.13 Generation-time rigging: Stroke3D, Rigel3D, Animator-Centric Skeletons (2026)

- **Stroke3D** (ICLR 2026, arXiv 2602.09713): from user-drawn 2D strokes plus text, generate a skeleton (latent diffusion) and then a textured mesh conditioned on it. Claims to be first stroke-conditioned rigged-mesh generator. No code link found.
- **Rigel3D** (arXiv 2605.13129, May 2026; Chatzis, Loizou, Kalogerakis): rather than rigging a finished mesh, it models geometry and rig in coupled structured latents, outputs mesh, skeleton, joints and skin weights, and uses an open-vocabulary joint-labelling module so joints map onto arbitrary retargeting templates. Trained on the Anymate training split. No code found.
- **Animator-Centric Skeleton Generation** (CVPR 2026, arXiv 2604.20539): 82,633 rigged meshes, semantic bone grouping in tokens, and a **learnable bone-density control** so an animator can ask for more or fewer bones. Release unstated.
**Why they matter.** They signal where image-to-3D is going: generating an animatable asset directly instead of mesh-then-rig. For Homunculus (which already uses Pixal3D for shape) this is a 2027 consideration, not a drop-in.

### 3.14 Rig-free: Animate3D (NeurIPS 2024) and AnimateAnyMesh (ICCV 2025)

**Animate3D** (Jiang et al., arXiv 2407.11398). **MV-VDM**, a multi-view video diffusion model conditioned on renderings of a static object, trained on **MV-Video** (about 115K animations, 1.8M multi-view videos); the generated multi-view videos give a coarse motion that is then refined with **4D score distillation sampling** (optimise motion so that renders look likely to a diffusion model). Output is an animated 3D representation, usually via Gaussians or a deformation field rather than a standard skeleton. Authors state code and data are open.
**AnimateAnyMesh** (Wu et al., HUST/DAMO, arXiv 2506.09982, ICCV 2025). **DyMeshVAE** compresses a mesh's vertex trajectories (initial position plus relative motion) into a latent space, using vertex connectivity from the faces to respect topology; a **rectified-flow** text-to-trajectory model (conditioned on text and the mesh) generates the motion in seconds. Trained on **DyMesh** (over 4M dynamic mesh sequences). Authors say code/models "will be released"; I did not confirm that they are.
**Why they matter.** No rigging needed, so they work on anything. **Costs:** the output is per-vertex motion, not a skeleton: you cannot drop in Mixamo clips, edit the animation as joint curves, or load it in Unity/Unreal as a skeletal mesh. Fine for look-dev videos and previews, not for an interactive character.
**Links.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11398 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09982

### 3.15 Rig-free pose transfer and AKD, SkelMo (2022-2026)

- **Skeleton-free Pose Transfer** (Liao et al., ECCV 2022, arXiv 2208.00790): predict skin weights and deformation transforms jointly from a source pose, for stylised characters with no rig. Later **TapMo** and **HMC (hierarchical mesh coarsening)** extend it to text-driven and coarse-handle-based motion (HMC admits failure on very different morphologies).
- **Articulated Kinematics Distillation** (arXiv 2504.01204): keeps the skeleton and distils video-diffusion motion into joint angles via SDS, which preserves shape better than neural deformation fields.
- **SkelMo** (arXiv 2606.01518, June 2026): diffusion model producing skeletal animation for any rigged shape from 2D video guidance, across animals and fantasy creatures, trained on about 20K animated 3D assets. Project page only.
- **3D Gaussian splatting**: my search found human-avatar methods that attach Gaussians to a skeleton (SplatArmor, 3DGS-Avatar) and a cage-based sketch deformation paper, but no mature generic rig-free Gaussian animator for arbitrary characters. A vendor note says you cannot rig a Gaussian splat directly; MIA's paper claims to accept splats. Treat generic Gaussian animation as **research, unverified for production**.

### 3.16 Retargeting: SAN, R2ET and the cross-topology wave

**Classic retargeting** copies joint rotations between two skeletons with the same bone structure and fixes proportions with IK (this is what Homunculus's `smpl_to_bvh` and retarget stage do for GVHMR's SMPL body onto Mixamo).
**SAN** (Aberman et al., TOG 2020): collapses different skeletons (that are "homeomorphic", can be reduced to the same simple skeleton by merging chain bones) into a shared "primal skeleton" via skeletal pooling, and retargets without paired data. Limitation: needs retraining per set of topologies and ignores mesh geometry.
**R2ET** (Zhang et al., CVPR 2023, code github.com/Kebii/R2ET): a skeleton-aware module keeps the source motion's semantics and a shape-aware module reduces **interpenetration** (hands through the body) and missing contact, as learned residual corrections; evaluated on Mixamo.
**Newer cross-topology work (2025-26, all papers, quality unverified):** **Motion2Motion** (arXiv 2508.13139) training-free, needs only a few target-skeleton examples and sparse bone correspondences, covers biped to quadruped; **MoReFlow** (2509.25600) flow-matching between two characters' motion spaces, evaluated on humanoid robots and a Spot quadruped; **PALUM** (2601.07272) part-based attention across topologies; **HuMoT** (2305.18897) topology-agnostic transformer; **ReActor** (RL-based physics retargeting, unverified, summary page only).
**Why.** Dance from video onto a four-legged or tailed character has no direct bone mapping; these are the methods to watch. Today none is turnkey.

### 3.17 Foot skate and IK clean-up

Foot skate arises because video-to-motion models regress joint angles per frame without a firm sense of contact. **GVHMR** (used by Homunculus, arXiv 2409.06662) reduces it by working in a gravity-aligned world frame, yet small sliding remains. Standard remedies: (1) detect foot-contact frames (velocity of heel/toe below threshold, or learned contact labels), (2) pin the foot in world space on those frames and solve leg **IK** so the foot stays put, (3) smooth the root trajectory. A 2011-era user study (cited by the footskate perceptual evaluation I found) says people notice even very low sliding, so it is worth doing. Related: a mechanical-model optimisation appears in StableMoFusion. I did not find an off-the-shelf IK-based foot-lock for GVHMR output; Blender's IK constraints with a contact mask are the practical route. (Details of any GVHMR test-time post-processing: **unverified**.)

### 3.18 Hands, fingers and faces

- **MANO**: a parametric hand model learned from about 1,000 scans of 31 subjects; combined with the SMPL body it forms **SMPL+H**. **SMPL-X** (Pavlakos et al., 2019, arXiv 1904.05866) merges body (SMPL), hands (MANO pose space) and face (FLAME expression space) in one model with 10,475 vertices and 54 joints including neck, jaw, eyes and fingers; it uses LBS plus learned corrective blend shapes. A CVPR 2026 supplement reports MANO fits scans a bit tighter than SMPL-X's hands (1.34 mm vs 1.98 mm). **NIMBLE** adds bones and muscles; a 2025 study found NIMBLE-based synthetic data beat MANO on FreiHAND.
- **FLAME** is the standard head model (shape, pose, expression); **ARKit's 52 blendshapes** are Apple's facial-expression standard (names such as jawOpen, eyeBlinkLeft). I did not find a source this session mapping ARKit to FLAME (background knowledge: the mapping is done by regression or artist sculpting, and glTF morph targets can carry the 52 shapes; **unverified**).
- **Why for Homunculus.** The rig's fingers are the weak point: auto-riggers place five finger chains per hand but weights on small, touching fingers are blurry, and generative meshes often fuse fingers (documented in the Homunculus repair stage). SMPL-X-style models do not help the *mesh*; they help the *motion source* (GVHMR already outputs SMPL body; hand poses come from a pose library).

### 3.19 Clothing, hair and secondary motion

**Secondary motion** is the jiggle that follows the main motion: hair, capes, tails, skirts. Three levels:
1. **Spring bones** (rigid chains with stiffness, gravity and drag, plus sphere/capsule colliders). Standardised in VRM as `VRMC_springBone`; in Blender the "wiggle bone" add-ons do similar. UniRig's README lists "bone attribute prediction" (the physics parameters) as coming soon. VRM 1.0 was released September 2022 per a Library of Congress entry.
2. **Cloth simulation** (position-based or finite element), offline; or **neural cloth**: Neural Cloth Simulation (Bertiche et al., 2022, unsupervised, separates static and dynamic subspaces), Deep Emulator for Secondary Motion (2021, user paints constraints and stiffness), and a GDC session reporting a neural replacement for hair/cloth/accessory physics at 0.02-0.13 ms per character on one CPU core.
3. **Neural hair**: HairFormer (arXiv 2507.12600, Jul 2025): a transformer predicts the static draped shape for a hairstyle, then a dynamic network adds motion, real-time, arbitrary hairstyles.
All are paper-grade; none plugs into a glTF rig out of the box.

### 3.20 File formats that matter

- **glTF/GLB**: skin weights stored as `JOINTS_0`/`WEIGHTS_0` with 4 influences per vertex by default. More influences can use extra sets (`_1`, ...), but support is uneven: an Unreal Engine 5.8 bug report says the Interchange importer keeps only four influences, and Epic replied it follows the spec; three.js reportedly uses only the first four. **Practical rule: limit skin weights to the 4 strongest influences per vertex and renormalise before export**, or you get silent deformation changes between tools.
- **FBX**: what Mixamo and MIA's Mixamo skeleton use; proprietary, converted via FBX2glTF or Blender. Puppeteer and UniRig export FBX.
- **VRM** (glTF-based): standard humanoid bone names (so any VRM animation works on any VRM avatar) plus spring bones plus expressions. A good target for avatar use; UniRig reads/writes it with an add-on.
- **USD (UsdSkel)**: Pixar's skeleton schema; I did not verify details this session. **Unverified**, mention only as the studio/Omniverse path.
- **BVH**: motion only. Homunculus's own bug D-16 (BVH columns in depth-first order) shows how easy it is to get channel order wrong.

## 4. What this means for Homunculus

### 4.1 Is Make-It-Animatable still the right rigger?

For **humanoids that must use Mixamo clips**, yes: it is the only option in this survey whose output *is* the Mixamo skeleton, which is what the animation stage, the Mixamo zip export and the Thriller clips depend on. It is fast (about 0.5 s claimed), MIT-licensed (repo sidebar), and v2 reportedly uses a stronger shape backbone (Hunyuan3D 2.1 ShapeVAE), which is worth trying: **evaluate MIA v2 vs v1 on the existing run set** (check the finger and wrist-bend checks) before anything else.

### 4.2 Alternatives, ranked by usefulness

| Option | Best for | Cost/risk |
|---|---|---|
| **SkinTokens/TokenRig** (MIT, ≥14 GB, 1.6 GB weights) | non-humanoids, props, odd proportions, one-model skeleton plus skin | outputs its own skeleton, not Mixamo; needs a retarget step; CUDA-oriented (ROCm unverified) |
| **UniRig** (MIT, ≥8 GB) | VRM/anime characters; accepts .vrm; FBX output | released weights are the smaller Articulation-XL2.0 set; superseded by SkinTokens |
| **MagicArticulate** (Apache-2.0, ~4.6 GB) | cheap skeleton proposals | skin weights weaker than newer work, dataset licence unclear |
| **Puppeteer** (Apache-2.0) | rig plus video-driven animation of non-humanoids | old CUDA 11.8 stack, bpy 4.2 pin |
| **Blender Automatic Weights + Rigify** | deterministic fallback; fixes | manual alignment |
| **Anymate baselines** | research baselines | licence unstated, 8-GPU training |
| Rigel3D, Stroke3D, Animator-Centric, RigAnything | future | no code found |

A sensible arrangement: keep MIA as the humanoid default; run SkinTokens as a *second opinion* on the same mesh and compare skin weights near the hands; use it as the primary route when the planner (Qwen VL) labels the character as non-humanoid.

### 4.3 Fixing hands and fingers

1. **Measure, don't guess**: Homunculus already has a wrist-bend check; add a finger-curl test (pose all five fingers to a fist, compute volume change, interpenetration and weight leakage between adjacent fingers).
2. **Compare riggers on hand vertices only**: MIA vs SkinTokens vs Blender auto-weights; pick per hand, or blend weights vertex-wise.
3. **Post-process weights**: limit to the strongest 4 influences, enforce that finger-bone weights stay inside each finger's geodesic region, smooth wrist weights. The learned methods predict smooth weights; a clean-up is cheap. A geodesic prior is exactly what MagicArticulate and Auto-Connect used to reduce artefacts.
4. **Fix geometry first**: fused fingers cause bad weights no matter the rigger, which the repair stage already addresses.
5. **Fist and gesture poses** come from a pose library already; consider adding corrective blend shapes at the knuckles (the Neural Blend Shapes idea), authored by Blender shape keys driven by finger bend.

### 4.4 Non-humanoid characters

The Mixamo skeleton cannot express tails, wings, extra limbs or quadruped gait. Path: (a) rig with SkinTokens/UniRig/Puppeteer (template-free); (b) for motion, either animate with Puppeteer-style video optimisation or retarget a humanoid/quadruped mocap clip using a cross-topology method (Motion2Motion needs only sparse bone correspondences and a few target examples; MoReFlow targets robots and Spot). Expect manual cleanup. Quadruped coverage in the open riggers is not well documented: no source I found tested quadrupeds directly (**unverified**), though RigAnything and SkelMo claim animals.

### 4.5 Props and accessories

Rigid props (a sword) should be parented to a hand bone (or an attachment socket) rather than skinned. Dangling things (cape, scarf, tail, ponytail) are chains: either add spring bones (VRM-style) or let SkinTokens/UniRig produce chain bones and drive them with Blender's wiggle bones. The UniRig bone-attribute predictor, if released, would estimate stiffness/damping per chain.

### 4.6 Cloth and hair

Cheapest valuable step: **spring-bone chains for hair, capes, skirts** driven at export or in the viewer (three-vrm supports `VRMC_springBone`, background knowledge, unverified). Neural simulators (HairFormer, Neural Cloth Simulation, the GDC talk) show direction but have no turnkey, ROCm-friendly code. For video renders Blender cloth simulation on the baked clip is reliable if slow.

### 4.7 Retargeting and foot-skate

Keep SMPL to Mixamo retargeting with the current floor-is-lowest-foot-joint rule; add a **foot-contact lock**: detect contact frames, pin feet, solve leg IK, and smooth the root. For multi-character scenes (O-4 in the decision log) this matters more: skating is very visible next to a second character.

### 4.8 Formats

Export GLB with ≤4 influences per vertex; keep FBX for Mixamo clips; consider VRM as the second target because it standardises bone names and spring bones. Do not choose rig-free output as the primary format: engines, Mixamo clips and the web viewer all expect skeletons.

### 4.9 ROCm risk

Every verified rigger lists CUDA wheels (CUDA 11.8, flash-attn, pytorch3d). Plan a smoke test per candidate on the AMD GPU before committing; MIA already runs there in this pipeline, which is a real asset. Flash-attention and pytorch3d are the usual trouble spots (background knowledge).

## 5. Open problems

1. **Fingers and thin parts**: still the least reliable region of every auto-rigger; no benchmark isolates it.
2. **Evaluation**: rigging accuracy (joint distance, skin L1) does not equal "looks good when animated"; UniRig's "motion accuracy" is a step, but nobody has a perceptual benchmark.
3. **Non-humanoid motion**: skeleton exists, but where does the motion come from? Cross-topology retargeting and video-to-skeleton (SkelMo, Puppeteer) are early, and most are papers without verified code.
4. **Data**: datasets come from Objaverse-style web scrapes with mixed licences; SkinTokens' and UniRig's full training sets are "to be released"; commercial use needs checking.
5. **Clothing layers and secondary motion**: no auto-rigger outputs physics parameters yet (UniRig's is "coming soon").
6. **Joint generation and rigging** (Rigel3D, Stroke3D) is promising but unreleased.
7. **Skinning quality under extreme poses**: LBS correctives are rarely learned in the generic riggers (only NBS tackled this directly).
8. **Open-vocabulary joint naming** so any rig maps to a retarget template (Rigel3D's idea) is not solved in released code.
9. **AMD/ROCm support** is nobody's priority in these repos.
