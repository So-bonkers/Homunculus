# Dance from a video

**In the web app:** the Animate page (`/ui#/animate`): pick any rigged character, drop a video, drag the range (or play it and press Start here / End here), Copy the dance. The job is followed as stages (cut, body motion, camera, retarget, texture, preview, side by side), each with its time and a report, and the time left (the estimates in `homunculus/stages/video_progress.py`, scaled by how fast the finished stages went). When it is done the reference and the character play together on one clock; scrub, slow down, orbit the 3D view. The same is in a run's Animate tab. The job waits for the GPU like the other jobs and adds a clip to the run (with a preview render and the video next to it). It does everything below, plus the camera step (a moving camera is tracked from the background and taken out of the dancer's path; a steady one is left alone). The rest of this page is the same pipeline by hand.

Video of one dancer -> SMPL motion (GVHMR) -> BVH (`tools/smpl_to_bvh.py`) -> your rigged character (`tools/retarget_bvh.py --map smpl`) -> preview (`tools/make_mp4.py --fixed`).

## One-time setup (everything lives in `gvhmr/`, which git ignores)
1. `git clone --depth 1 https://github.com/zju3dv/GVHMR gvhmr/repo`
2. `uv venv --python 3.12 gvhmr/.venv`, then PyTorch for ROCm (`uv pip install torch torchvision --index-url https://download.pytorch.org/whl/rocm7.2`) and the packages GVHMR imports (timm, lightning, pytorch-lightning, hydra-core, hydra-zen, hydra_colorlog, rich, numpy<2, scipy, einops, imageio, av, opencv-python, scikit-image, trimesh, smplx, ultralytics, cython_bbox, lapx, yacs, ...). The pinned CUDA 12.1 build in GVHMR's requirements is not used.
3. Body models need a free, non-commercial registration: SMPL (smpl.is.tue.mpg.de, version 1.1.0 with the neutral model) and SMPL-X (smpl-x.is.tue.mpg.de). Put `SMPLX_NEUTRAL.npz` in `gvhmr/repo/inputs/checkpoints/body_models/smplx/`. The SMPL `.pkl` was pickled with chumpy: strip it to plain numpy with a restricted unpickler and save it as `body_models/smpl/SMPL_NEUTRAL.pkl`.
4. Checkpoints (dpvo, gvhmr, hmr2, vitpose, yolo) from GVHMR's Google Drive folder; the ViTPose file hit Drive's quota, the mirror `camenduru/GVHMR` on HuggingFace has identical files.
5. `gvhmr/shim/pytorch3d`: the pure-torch rotation code copied from pytorch3d plus a small `knn`, and stubs for the renderer (not available on ROCm; rendering is done in Blender).

## Per video
```
ffmpeg -ss 30 -t 20 -i video.mp4 -vf fps=30 -an -c:v libx264 -crf 14 gvhmr/work/clip.mp4       # the model expects 30 fps; cut where the camera does not cut
gvhmr/.venv/bin/python tools/video_to_smpl.py --video gvhmr/work/clip.mp4 --output_root gvhmr/work/out -s      # -s = fixed camera
python3 -I tools/smpl_to_bvh.py gvhmr/work/out/clip/smpl_global.npz gvhmr/work/clip.bvh
blender -b --python tools/retarget_bvh.py -- <character.fbx> gvhmr/work/clip.bvh gvhmr/work/rt clip --map smpl --fps 30
.venv/bin/python tools/make_mp4.py gvhmr/work/rt/clip.fbx gvhmr/work/rt/clip.mp4 --fixed
```
A 20 s clip took about two and a half minutes on the Radeon (YOLO tracking, ViTPose, HMR2 features, then the model itself: under 2 s).

## What to expect
The dance's heading is not recoverable from the clip: if the character faces away from the render camera, pass `--yaw 180` (any angle) to `smpl_to_bvh.py`. For the first test clip, where the dancer faces the camera at the start, 180 is right.

Body only (no fingers), depth is guessed from one camera, feet slide (0.6-0.7 body heights per second while low in the first test), fast spins and floor work are weaker. Tested on one 20 s freestyle slice (dark clothes, coloured light, steady camera): the arm and leg poses matched the footage at five checked moments.

## Fingers (work in progress, 2026-10-14)
GVHMR gives the body only, so a dance from a video leaves every finger at rest. The hand-pose layer (`homunculus/blender_handpose.py`) can now give each hand a **timeline of poses** (open, relaxed, fist, grip, point, thumbs up, pinch; eased between), not one pose for the whole clip:

```json
{"tracks": {"left": [[0, "open"], [2.0, "fist"], [5.0, "point"]], "right": [[0, "fist"], [3.0, "open"]]}, "ease": 0.13, "ramp": 6, "lib": "<handpose.LIB>"}
```
```bash
blender -b --python homunculus/blender_handpose.py -- clip.glb spec.json      # rewrites the GLB (and the FBX next to it)
```
Times are in seconds from the start of the clip. Tried on the Thriller rig with a hand-made track: open, fist, point, grip and thumbs-up all show on both hands at the set times.

**The timeline editor** (Animate tab, under the video and the character of a dance from a video): one lane per hand, a block per pose. Play the video and press **1-7** (open, relaxed, fist, grip, point, thumbs up, pinch) at the moment a hand changes; **L / R / B** choose the left, right or both hands; click a block to change or remove it, drag its left edge to move the change. **Apply to the model** saves the timeline (`runs/<run>/09_animate/hands/<clip>.json`: these are also the labels of a later classifier) and bakes it into a copy of the clip (`<clip>_hands.glb`, about 10 s on the CPU); the original clip is untouched, and an empty timeline takes the fingers off again. API: `GET /api/hands/<run>/<clip>`, `POST /api/hands`. Code: `homunculus/hands.py`, `web/src/features/animate/HandTimeline.tsx`.

**The label library** (`homunculus/handshapes.py`): 48 hand shapes in seven groups (neutral, curved, closed, pointing, signs, pinch and snap, finger wave), each defined the way sign-language handshapes are: a state per finger (straight, soft, curved, claw, hook, bent, half, fist, tight), a thumb position (side, out, up, across, oppose, tucked) and how far the fingers are spread. Every shape has a picture (side view and top view, `static/hands/`; redraw with `python -m homunculus.hand_library <rig.fbx|glb>`, on a Mixamo character: the project's own Make-It-Animatable rigs orient the finger bones differently and curl backwards, so they are not used). The editor shows nine pinned poses on keys 1-9 (star a pose in the picker to pin it), an **All poses** picker with search and categories, and **Gestures**: snap, flick, clench-release, burst, finger wave, jazz-hands shake and finger tap, dropped at the playhead as a timed run of poses (timings are animation estimates, editable afterwards). Each change has its own **transition**: smooth (minimum jerk, 0.13 s), quick (0.07 s), snap (0.05 s with a little overshoot), linear or cut, and an optional length in milliseconds; a change after a short pose is shortened so a quick accent still arrives. See [research/08](research/08_hand_label_vocabularies.md) for the vocabularies this is based on (no published vocabulary ships joint angles: the shared scheme is finger states + thumb + spread) and the timing facts (peak finger speed about 1,300 degrees a second, so a full curl takes at least ~70 ms).

**Wrist and palm direction** are two more kinds of label, each with a lane per hand under the finger lanes (switch with Fingers / Wrist / Palm, or the keys **F / W / P**; the pins on keys 1-9 follow the kind). The **wrist** is how the hand bends on the forearm, in degrees within the human range: in (toward the palm) and back, sideways toward the thumb or the little finger, and the combinations (15 positions, drawn in the picker); it is added on top of what the video gave. The **palm direction** is the forearm's roll, i.e. the elbow's twist: which way the palm faces (down, up, forward, back, in, out). It is absolute: while a direction is on, the palm faces it as closely as the arm allows (a palm cannot face along its own forearm), and "As the clip" gives it back to the motion from the video. GVHMR observes a forearm's twist and a wrist's bend only weakly, so these are where the annotator's eye does better than the video. The elbow's *bend* is already in the dance (GVHMR estimates it with the body). Wrist and palm gestures (wrist flick, wave, circle) are in the Gestures menu of the Wrist lane.

**Rigs.** The finger, wrist and palm layers work out each hand's own frame from its bones (the palm normal from the hand axis and the thumb side, mirrored for the right hand), so they hold for Mixamo characters (palms down in the T-pose) and for the pipeline's own Make-It-Animatable rigs (palms forward); the bend of a joint at rest is the zero of its curl, so "straight" is straight on any rig.

Where the poses come from automatically is the open part. In a wide shot of a group the hands are about 40 px (1080p video), blurred and partly hidden, so a hand-pose estimator cannot read the fingers there; a close shot (a hand over ~150 px) may. Planned: a classifier (open / fist / point ...) trained on the editor's labels plus synthetic hands (the rig's hand rendered in a known pose, drawn realistic under a depth map, degraded to 40 px), and the hand boxes saved per frame so the labels come with their crops.

## Automatic hand labels: what was tried (2026-10-14)
One 20 s clip (the Kinjaz centre dancer) was annotated by hand in all six lanes: about 200 changes, over 30 minutes. Questions, and what the clip's data says:

| Question | Result |
|---|---|
| Does the video's own motion (GVHMR) carry the wrist and the palm direction? | No. The retargeted wrist never bends (range 0 to 0). The clip's palm direction picks the annotator's label on 11 % (left) and 7 % (right) of frames; guessing the most common label gets 49 % and 43 %. |
| Can a classifier on the hand pixels learn the labels from 20 s? | No. 64 px hand crops, DINOv2-small features, a linear head trained on the first 13 s, tested on the last 6 s: fingers 19 % / 17 %, wrist 62 % / 40 %, palm 50 % / 28 %, all at or below guessing the commonest label. |
| Can a body-motion prior (trained on FineDance, 155 files, 5.7 h) guess the hands from the arms? | Palm direction: 58 % on held-out FineDance files against 33 % for guessing, and 45 % / 41 % on the clip (the clip's own palm: about 10 %; the annotator's habit: 49 % / 43 %). Finger closure: no signal (49 % against 48 %). The wrist bend of FineDance is zero too, so a model of body motion cannot supply it. |
| Do the changes happen on the beat, on sounds, or on sharp arm movements? | Not on sounds or the (crudely estimated) beats. Slightly on sharp wrist movements: 40 % within 70 ms against 30 % for random times, 56 % within 120 ms against 42 %. |
| Can a vision-language model label them? (Qwen3.8-27B, thinking on, 14 000 tokens; October 2026) | No. Per 2 s window it got the dancer plus a strip of wrist-centred hand crops (ViTPose wrist and elbow, 5 Hz, upscaled to 224 px) and the coarse classes; about 90 s per window. Against your labels on two clips (Jabbawockeez stage a, 7 s; Kinjaz centre dancer, 19 s; 2 of 14 windows came back as broken JSON): fingers 34 % against 39 % for always guessing the commonest label (kappa 0.12), wrist 56 % against 63 % (kappa 0.07), palm 21 % against 53 % (kappa 0.00). When it says it can see the hand it is no better. Many crops contain no hand (a sleeve, the background). One trial, one prompt; not tuned. |
| Which settings make the model's labelling cheaper without ruining it? (the Kinjaz centre-dancer clip, 19.3 s, full library vocabulary, no repeat of the baseline so no noise floor) | Seconds to label the clip, and how often the cheaper run gives the same label as the same model's 25 fps thinking-on run (mean of six lanes, coarse classes). Qwen3.8 27B: baseline 642 s; 12.5 fps 379 s (58 %); thinking off 452 s (48 %); both 222 s (42 %). Qwen3.6 35B-A3B: baseline 831 s (it crashed once with speculative decoding and was retried); 12.5 fps 478 s (63 %); thinking off 285 s (59 %); both 161 s (54 %). The two models agree with each other on wrist and palm (55-71 %) but not on fingers (about 30 %, 11-12 % by exact name). Every run agrees with the person's own labels at 31-42 %. |
| How much does one run of the model differ from another with the same settings? (Qwen3.8 27B, 12.5 fps, thinking off, three runs on the Kinjaz centre-dancer clip) | A lot. The three runs agree with each other on 36-46 % of frames (22-38 % by exact name), as little as the differences between settings above, which are therefore inside the noise. How often the labels change varies as much: 40, 87 and 105 changes on the left-finger lane (the person's own labels: 39). Time is steady (222, 228, 216 s). The person judged the second run the best of all runs so far (the 25 fps thinking-on baseline and the first 12.5 fps thinking-off run were also among the best); neither agreement with the baseline nor the number of label changes predicts which runs the person prefers. A single run is one draw; comparing settings needs several runs each, judged by eye. |

So: wrist and finger labels need the hand pixels, and the hands are about 64 px in a wide shot; the palm direction can be drafted from the arm geometry; 20 s is far too little to train a pixel model. The routes that remain are more labels (made cheaper by editor shortcuts), synthetic training data rendered from the dancer's own model, and a close-up estimator (HaMeR) where a hand is large.

**Sound and drafts in the editor.** The reference video's sound is read in the browser and shown as a strip above the lanes (envelope, with a tick where the sound jumps); the player has a volume slider, **M** mutes and **O** loops 2 s from just before the playhead at full speed with sound, so a change can be placed by ear. A reference file with no sound says so. **Drafts:** `tools/register_track_clip.py` puts any tracked dancer of a shot (tools/multi_track.py) on a rig as a clip with a cropped reference and the shot's sound; the labels of a dancer already annotated can be copied across where the dancers are in unison (forearm directions agree 0.80 between the Kinjaz dancers 5 and 4, 0.67 for 3), saved with a note (`meta` in `hands/<clip>.json`) and shown as an "auto draft: check it" badge until a person saves. A person's own save has no note, so the badge goes away.

**Testing the palm prior on a new stretch.** `homunculus/hand_prior.py` (weights in `homunculus/data/hand_prior.npz`, trained by `tools/train_hand_prior.py` on FineDance, pure numpy at run time) proposes the palm direction from the arms' geometry. `tools/draft_palm.py <run> <clip> <smpl_global.npz>` saves its proposal for a clip as an unreviewed draft in the two palm lanes (nothing predicts the fingers or the wrist, so those stay empty) and keeps it apart. After a person labels the palm lanes of that clip and presses Apply, the editor shows **model vs you**: the share of the clip where the labels kept the model's choice, next to what always guessing the person's own most common label would score. The first test clip is a different shot from the one the prior was checked on: the Kinjaz solo, 0:06-0:22 (16.6 s; after that the shot changes to a group), where the dancer fills about two thirds of the frame.
